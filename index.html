Australian public libraries, 2017 to 2025

From the Shelf to the Screen

Australians have not stopped using libraries. They borrow differently, visit differently, and the pandemic changed who walks through the door.

The big picture

One in three loans now happens on a screen

In 2024-25, libraries across Australia recorded about 180 million loans. A digital loan means an e-book or audiobook borrowed on a phone, a streaming session, or a search of a library database.

Two thirds of all library use is still physical. But digital grew far faster, and the next chart shows how quickly.

Chapter 1: The shift

Digital borrowing rose by 42% in three years

Between 2021-22 and 2024-25, physical loans grew from 103 million to 119 million. Digital use went from 43 million to 61 million, lifting its share of all library use from 30% to 34%.

The doors reopened, and the website stayed busy

Library visits collapsed during the pandemic, then recovered strongly. In-person visits rose from 56 million in 2021-22 to 91 million in 2024-25. That is a strong recovery, but still below the 111 million recorded in 2017-18. Website visits also climbed, so many people now use both.

Chapter 2: Where

Digital use depends on where you live

The act has the highest digital share at 44%, and Victoria the lowest at 29%. Queensland is close to the top at 40%.

The ACT is too small to see on this map. The tiles in the next chart show it clearly.

Visits tell a different story

In South Australia, 61% of library visits are to the website. In New South Wales it is 34%. Each state is drawn as an equal square so that small places are not lost.

Branches follow people

New South Wales has 365 of Australia's 1,409 library branches, Queensland has 303 and Victoria 292. The ACT has 10.

Chapter 3: Change

Every state changed, but not in the same way

Comparing 2021-22 with 2024-25 shows where in-person visits bounced back, where digital grew fastest, and where programs expanded.

Queensland stopped collecting from some small services, so its changes are not directly comparable. The ACT's website visits fell by about four fifths, and the reports do not say why, so treat that figure with caution.

Chapter 4: The people

Fewer Australians went to a library in 2021-22 than in 2017-18

A national survey asks people whether they went to a venue in the last year. Library attendance among adults fell from 31% to 20% between 2017-18 and 2021-22, and among children from 53% to 33%.

The 2021-22 survey covers July 2020 to June 2022, so lockdowns affect these figures.

Libraries held up better than most venues

Cinemas and live music lost more than 20 percentage points. Libraries lost about 10. Among Australians aged 75 and over, libraries became the most visited venue of all.

The regulars are still there

A quarter of adult library visitors went eleven or more times in 2021-22, down from a third in 2017-18. Women visit more than men: 25% of women went to a library, against 16% of men.

Fewer Australians are library members than a few years ago, yet visits and digital borrowing have both grown.

About this visualisation

Why and who

This page is for Australians curious about how libraries are changing. It explains the shift from print to digital and the effect of the pandemic, without needing any statistics knowledge.

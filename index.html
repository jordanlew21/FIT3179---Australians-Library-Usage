Australian public libraries, 2017 to 2025

From the Shelf to the Screen

Australians still use libraries a lot. They borrow on screens, visit in person again, and join less often than before.

The big picture

One in three loans is now digital

Libraries logged about 180 million loans in 2024-25, roughly 6.6 for every Australian. A digital loan is an e-book, audiobook, stream or database search.

Two thirds are still physical. The other third is the part growing fastest.

Chapter 1: The shift

Digital use is up 42% in three years

Digital use grew from 43 million to 61 million between 2021-22 and 2024-25, lifting its share from 30% to 34%. Physical loans rose by 16 million too, so screens are adding to print, not replacing it.

The doors reopened, and the website stayed busy

In-person visits rebounded from 56 million to 91 million, about 82% of the 2017-18 level of 111 million. Another 70 million visits went to library websites, so libraries now have two front doors.

Chapter 2: The catch

Busier libraries, fewer members

Visits, loans and programs are all above 2021-22. Membership is the one measure going the other way, from 8.4 million to 7.8 million people, or 32.6% to 28.8% of the population.

The report says part of the fall comes from changed definitions and family cards.

A national survey sees fewer people going out

The ABS asks whether people went to a venue in the past year. Adults who visited a library fell from 31% to 20%, and children from 53% to 33%. Culture-going dropped at every age, from 82% of adults to 64%.

Library statistics count visits, while the survey counts people. That is how visits can recover while fewer people go.

The 2021-22 survey covers July 2020 to June 2022, so lockdowns affect these figures.

Libraries lost a third of their audience, like cinemas

Libraries, cinemas and art galleries each lost about a third. Live music and theatre lost about half, so libraries held up better than most.

The regulars are still there

A quarter of adult library visitors went 11 or more times in 2021-22, down from a third in 2017-18. Women are the bigger group: 25% went, against 16% of men.

Chapter 3: Where

Digital use depends on where you live

The ACT leads at 44% digital and Victoria trails at 29%. Every state except the ACT became more digital since 2021-22, and the Northern Territory jumped from 21% to 36%.

The ACT is too small to see on this map, so the map on the right circles it.

Visits tell a different story. In South Australia 61% of visits are to the website, against 34% in New South Wales.

Branches follow people

New South Wales has 365 of Australia's 1,409 branches, Queensland 303 and Victoria 292. The ACT has 10.

Each circle sits on the state's capital but counts the whole state, and its area is proportional to branches. Hover to see the count.

Packed in the south-east, thin elsewhere

Victoria has about 12 libraries for every 10,000 square kilometres. Western Australia has fewer than one, and the Northern Territory one for every 44,000.

Each dot is one of 1,440 libraries at its real location (2016–2018 data). The interior is almost empty.

Chapter 4: Change

Every state changed, but not in the same way

Since 2021-22, New South Wales visits more than doubled (+120%) and the ACT's program numbers nearly quadrupled (+291%). The Northern Territory's visits barely moved (+8%).

Queensland stopped collecting from some small services, so its changes are not directly comparable. The ACT's website visits fell by about four fifths and the reports do not say why, so treat that figure with caution.

Did digital growth cost libraries their visitors?

No clear pattern says so. Victoria grew on both counts, New South Wales saw visits surge while digital rose 41%, and the Northern Territory grew fastest online (+73%) with visits flat.

Each dot is a state. Dashed lines mark the national change.

Visits are compared with 2021-22, when libraries were still affected by the pandemic, so large increases partly reflect reopening.

Chapter 5: What libraries do now

Libraries are becoming places to learn

Program participants doubled from 3.4 million to 7.0 million, with about 1,200 sessions run every day. Literacy and lifelong learning make up 64% of participants, and wellbeing programs more than tripled.

Digital inclusion programs, such as help getting online, were the only group to shrink in the last year. The reports do not say why. This breakdown was not collected for 2020-21.

Fewer people hold a card, but libraries are busier: more on screens, more for learning, and more than just books.

About this visualisation

Why and who

This page is for Australians curious about how libraries are changing. It explains the shift from print to digital and the effect of the pandemic, without needing any statistics knowledge.
